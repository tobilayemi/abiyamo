# Abiyamo: Users and Customers

Abiyamo has two groups of stakeholders, and they are not the same people.

- **Users** interact with Abiyamo directly, at the clinic or on their phone.
- **Customers** pay for Abiyamo or decide to buy it.

## At a glance

| Group | Who | What they do with Abiyamo |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary user | Midwives and nurses at primary health centres (PHCs) | Fill the ANC form at every visit and act on the risk report |
| User | Community health extension workers (CHEWs) and community health officers (CHOs) | Same form, where no midwife is available; rely on the referral decision |
| User | Pregnant women and new mothers | WhatsApp Mother Guide and Newborn Watch (in development) |
| Supporting user | Officer-in-charge of the facility | Follows up high-risk mothers and referrals (summary view planned) |
| **First customer type** | **State primary health care boards and state ministries of health** | Decide which tools public PHCs use; buy per-facility subscriptions |
| Pilot funder | Donor-funded maternal and newborn health programmes | Funds the six-month pilot |
| Later customers | Other states' PHC boards and ministries of health, private and faith-based hospitals, HMOs | Buy subscriptions for their facilities |

## Users

### 1. Midwife or nurse at a PHC (primary user)

> **Blessing, 34.** Nursing officer and midwife at a ward-level PHC. She runs the weekly ANC clinic and is often the only midwife on shift. She works on her own Android phone, and her mobile data drops several times a day.

**How she uses Abiyamo.** During each ANC visit she copies what is on the ANC card into the web form, one section per screen: the mother's details, pregnancy history, vital signs, test results and danger signs. She checks the review page and submits. Before the mother leaves, Blessing has a risk level, the reasons for it, the immediate actions and a referral decision.

**What she needs**
- Fast entry on a small screen, in the order she already examines the mother.
- No lost work when the network drops.
- An immediate warning when a value is dangerous, and a catch when a value is mistyped.
- Clear actions and a referral decision she can explain to a doctor.
- No extra paperwork on top of the ANC register.

### 2. Community health extension worker (CHEW) or CHO

> **Musa, 29.** CHEW at a health post with no midwife. He is trained in routine ANC but not in obstetric emergencies. He decides which mothers must travel to the general hospital, a trip that costs the family money.

**How he uses Abiyamo.** The same ANC form. He relies most on the danger-signs step, the referral decision, and the "why this result" list, which shows which entry raised the alert. He uses that list to explain the referral to the family and to the receiving facility.

**What he needs**
- Plain language, not specialist terms.
- Confidence to refer early, and to not refer when it is safe.
- Guidance that does not assume a doctor is on site.

### 3. Pregnant woman or new mother

> **Aisha, 23.** Second pregnancy. She lives a long bus ride from the PHC, shares a smartphone with her husband and prefers Pidgin.

**How she uses Abiyamo.** She does not use the web app. At booking, the midwife registers her phone number with her consent. She receives appointment reminders and danger-sign messages on WhatsApp, and can report symptoms by replying. After the birth she receives the Day 1 to 28 newborn checklist.

**What she needs**
- Messages in her language.
- Low data cost.
- Privacy on a shared phone.
- A clear "go to the clinic now" message when she reports a danger sign.

*Status: the WhatsApp Mother Guide and Newborn Watch are in development and are planned for the second half of the pilot.*

### Supporting user: officer-in-charge

Reviews the list of high-risk mothers each week and checks that referrals were completed. Needs a simple facility summary (planned).

## Customers

### First customer type: state primary health care boards

**Who they are.** The state agencies (and state ministries of health) that run public primary health care. They decide which tools, training and protocols their PHCs use. The buying decision sits with their leadership and their maternal and child health teams.

**Why they would pay**
- There are too few midwives for the number of pregnancies. Abiyamo lets nurses and CHEWs screen every ANC visit to the same standard.
- Referrals happen earlier and come with a documented reason.
- The Board gets facility-level data on high-risk pregnancies and referrals, the information it already reports (DHIS2 integration is planned).
- It runs on phones staff already carry, with no new hardware.

**How they would buy.** A per-facility annual subscription covering the software, training and support, once the pilot shows results. Pricing will be set from the pilot's actual costs.

### Pilot funder: donor-funded maternal and newborn health programmes

**Who they are.** Foundations, development partners and NGOs that run maternal and newborn health projects in Nigeria.

**Why they would pay.** They need low-cost interventions that show measurable results. Abiyamo produces those numbers for every facility: ANC visits screened, high-risk pregnancies found and referrals completed.

**How they would buy.** A grant covers the first pilot (licences, training and support), with a plan to hand over to the state primary health care board.

### Next customers, after the pilot

- **Private and faith-based hospitals:** a subscription that raises the quality of their ANC and reduces late emergencies.
- **HMOs:** fewer costly complications among enrolled mothers.

## First pilot

A first pilot with a small number of primary health centres is planned. The site, partner and pilot design are still being confirmed.
